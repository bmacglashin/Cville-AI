# Discovery / Fit-Call Script (20 minutes)

Goal: mutual qualification — NOT selling the build. You're deciding whether their $950 will be
well spent. Review `CLIENT_RED_FLAGS.md` the night before.

## Minute 0–2 · Frame it

> "Thanks for the time. Twenty minutes, two purposes: I want to understand where your week
> actually goes, and we'll both figure out whether a paid audit makes sense. If it doesn't,
> I'll say so and try to point you somewhere useful. Fair?"

(That last line does more selling than anything else you'll say.)

## Minute 2–12 · The five questions (listen 80%)

1. **"Walk me through what eats your week — what can only YOU do right now?"**
   *Listening for: proposals, pricing, approvals, answer-desk duty. The owner-as-COO signal.*
2. **"What happened to the last three inquiries that didn't close?"**
   *Listening for: follow-up gaps, no system, "huh, good question."*
3. **"When a job moves from sold to production, what travels — and what gets lost?"**
   *Listening for: handoff pain, rework stories, margin leaks.*
4. **"Where does your business knowledge live? If your best person won the lottery tomorrow,
   what walks out the door?"**
   *Listening for: documents vs. heads ratio; data readiness; sensitivity flags.*
5. **"What have you already tried with AI, and what made you stop?"**
   *Listening for: realistic expectations, burned-before skepticism (fine), magic-wand
   expectations (red flag).*

Quantify as you go: "How many proposals a month? How long does each take? What's a typical job
worth?" — these numbers become the audit's ROI math.

## Minute 12–16 · Reflect & frame the audit

> "Here's what I heard: ⟨two or three specifics with their numbers⟩. That's exactly the shape
> of problem the audit is built for. Two weeks: structured intake, a 90-minute interview like
> this but deeper, then you get an opportunity map with every use case scored — impact, effort,
> risk, data-readiness — a data-risk ranking, your top five, and a 30-day roadmap. $950 as a
> founding client; $500 comes back as credit if we implement together within two weeks of the
> readout. And if the right answer is 'fix the process first, AI later,' that's what it'll say."

State the boundary proactively (builds more trust than any pitch):

> "One thing I'm strict about: no regulated data — health records, personnel files — and
> nothing customer-facing goes out without a human approving it. If you're looking for a robot
> that talks to your customers unsupervised, I'm the wrong guy."

## Minute 16–20 · Close honestly

**Strong fit:** "I think this is worth your $950 — and ⟨specific workflow⟩ is probably where it
lands, though the audit will check my homework. I'll send the invoice and the intake link today;
which Tuesday/Thursday morning works for the interview?"

**Unclear:** "I want to think about whether ⟨concern⟩ makes this premature. I'll email you
tomorrow either way with a straight answer." (Then actually do it.)

**Not a fit:** "I don't think the audit pays for itself for you right now, because ⟨reason⟩.
If ⟨condition⟩ changes, call me. Meanwhile try ⟨one genuinely useful free pointer⟩." Ask for a
referral — see `REFERRAL_SCRIPT.md`. A no that refers two owners beats a bad yes.

## After every call (5 min, non-negotiable)

`/admin` → client/lead → add `discovery_notes` (call_type: fit_call) → update pipeline stage →
schedule the follow-up task. Notes rot in 24 hours.
