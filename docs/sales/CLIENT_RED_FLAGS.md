# Client Red Flags

Read the night before every fit call. With five founding slots, one wrong client costs 20% of
the cohort, a reference, and the calendar margin that protects your W2. **A no that refers two
owners beats a bad yes.**

## 🚫 Walk away (decline the audit)

1. **Wants regulated-data workflows** — health/student/tenant-screening/lending/payroll
   decisioning. *"That's outside what I'll build, full stop. For that you need ⟨compliance-
   specialized referral⟩."*
2. **Wants autonomy** — "the AI should just handle customers." The human-review line isn't a
   preference; it's the practice. If they sigh at it, they'll fight every checkpoint.
3. **Replace-employees framing** — "can this let me cut my office staff?" Wrong premise, ugly
   engagements, and your brand in a small town is the people who work for your clients.
4. **Wants to skip the audit** — "just build the thing." That's how shelf-ware happens, and the
   audit-first rule is only credible if it has no exceptions. (Persistent skippers: quote
   standard pricing, audit still required. Watch them find $950 of patience.)
5. **Payment friction at $950** — haggling the founding price or "after we see results."
   Payment behavior at the cheapest step predicts payment behavior at $3,500.
6. **Can't name a decision-maker** — committee buying at a 15-person company means nobody
   wants it. The OWNER books the interview or there's no engagement.

## ⚠️ Proceed with eyes open (price/scope accordingly)

7. **Chaos all the way down** — no digital anything, every process improvised. The audit still
   helps but the honest readout is "process first, AI second visit." Set that expectation ON
   the fit call so the deliverable doesn't disappoint.
8. **AI-burned and bitter** — fine if they're skeptical-but-curious; risky if they want you to
   fail to confirm the grudge. Test: do they describe what they'd LIKE to be true?
9. **Magic-wand vocabulary** — "I want it to just know…" Calibrate live with one concrete
   example of what current AI does/doesn't do; if recalibration doesn't stick, see #2.
10. **Owner has no 90 minutes** — reschedules the interview twice before paying. The interview
    IS the audit; no interview, no engagement.
11. **The data is one person's head and that person isn't in the room** — viable only if the
    knowledge-holder joins the interview. Get that commitment before invoicing.
12. **Growth-stage chaos** (doubling headcount, mid-acquisition) — real money but everything
    you map changes in 90 days. Scope the audit to the stable core only.

## Green flags (for contrast — what "yes" looks like)

Owner answers the five discovery questions with specifics and numbers · already writes things
down (even messily) · asks about data safety unprompted · laughs in recognition at "unofficial
COO" · has lost a job to a slower-than-competitor proposal and KNOWS it · says "my team would
love this" (not "my team will deal with it").

## The capacity red flag (yours, not theirs)

If taking this client means >2 audits in flight or touching the W2 firewall — it's a no
regardless of fit. "I'm delivery-capped until ⟨month⟩; want the first slot then?" Scarcity
told plainly is premium positioning, and it's also just true.
