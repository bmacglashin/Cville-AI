## The strategic read

The central tension: **a $100k run-rate in 12 months, from a cold network, part-time, without a
public founder.** Most plans would resolve it with content marketing and a loud personal brand —
both blocked here. The resolution is that Charlottesville is small and in-person:

> **Publicly quiet, privately everywhere.** The firm brand carries the website, the artifacts,
> and the paper. Ben carries the handshake. The goal is to be *privately famous* — known
> face-to-face by a few hundred owners and twenty connectors — while having no searchable
> public footprint.

The identity policy that falls out of this, applied everywhere:

- **Public surfaces** (website, printed one-pagers, the annual report, anything forwardable to
  strangers): firm voice only. "Founder-led" stays; the name, photo, and named credentials go.
- **Private channels** (1:1 email, fit calls, interviews, proposals, signed MSAs): full
  identity. A first-name email signature is normal business behavior, not a public profile.

Two honest caveats, stated because the prompt demands them:

1. **Low profile buys time; it is not a strategy.** If the conflict risk is real, the plan
   cannot depend on the employer never noticing — VA SCC filings are public record, and
   Charlottesville is small. The attorney consult (Decision #1) is about replacing hope with
   certainty: either the agreement permits this (and low profile is just prudence), or it
   doesn't (and the plan changes fundamentally). Everything else in this portfolio assumes the
   consult clears it.
2. **The $100k bar is reachable but has no slack.** The math below works only if audit →
   implementation conversion lands ≥40–50% and Operate attach holds. If those come in low, year
   1 is $50–70k and the W2 exit slips to month 18–24. The month-4 and month-8 gates exist to
   tell us early which world we're in. "Let evidence decide" was the answer; the gates are the
   evidence.

## The 12-month arc and the math

**Month-12 target state** (run-rate, not cumulative): 5 Operate retainers ($7.5k MRR ≈ $90k/yr)
+ ~1 standard audit/month + ~1 Quickstart/quarter ≈ **$105–115k run-rate**. Cumulative year-1
collected lands around $45–65k.

**The funnel to get there** (and the honest re-rating): the sales kit's 50 sends → 10–12 calls
assumes warm-market response. Cold, hyper-personalized local email realistically books 4–8%,
so 50 sends ≈ 2–4 calls. The engine therefore needs ~3× the touch volume *plus* the in-person
circuit, which converts far better per hour for a trust sale:

| Channel (60-day window) | Volume | Expected fit calls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold email (permit-sourced personalization) | 100–150 sends | 4–8 |
| In-person circuit (chamber, builders' assoc., supplier counters) | ~2 rooms/week | 6–10 |
| Connectors (CPAs, attorneys, brokers, bankers) | 20 met 1:1 | 3–6 |
| **Total** | | **13–24 → 3–5 paid audits** |

**Quarterly shape:** Q1 — launch ops complete, sprint run, 3 paid audits, ≥1 implementation
proposal out (exit MRR $0–1.5k). Q2 — cohort 2 at standard pricing, 2–3 implementations, first
1–2 Operate retainers, survey data collection starts (exit MRR $1.5–3k). Q3 — referral + connector
flow at ~1–1.5 audits/mo, 3–4 Operate cumulative, publish the firm-bylined report if data
supports it; month-8 gate (exit MRR $4–5.5k). Q4 — 5–6 Operate, price review, W2-exit /
first-hire decision package (exit run-rate $95–115k in the base case).

**Unit economics per rung** (founding-cohort prices; hours from the delivery playbook, items
marked *unproven* must be logged from audit #1):

| Rung | Price | Founder-hrs | ≈$/hr | Role in the model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audit (founding) | $950 | 10–12 | $85 | Paid discovery + method data; a marketing price |
| Audit (standard) | $1,250 | 10–12 | $110 | The permanent front door |
| Workflow Quickstart | $3,500 | 15–20 | $185 | Proof we implement, not just advise |
| Managed Workspace setup | $8,500 | 30–40 *(unproven)* | $240 | The bridge into Operate |
| Operate retainer | $1,500+/mo | 4–6/mo *(unproven)* | $300 | The run-rate engine; everything ladders here |

**Capacity check** (the scarce asset): month-12 steady state ≈ 5 Operate (25h) + 1 audit (11h)
+ selling (12h) + admin (6h) ≈ **54h/month ≈ 13h/week** — inside the 12–20 band but with no
slack, which is why pricing evolution and the first-hire trigger are a Next workstream, not
Later. During the sprint: GTM 8–10h/wk + ≤2 audits in flight obeys the playbook's tripwires.

**Moat (what compounds):** the playbook library priced from actuals, the vendor-vetting
dataset, the local referral graph, permit-sourced market intelligence, and eventually the
annual report dataset. None of it depends on the founder being public.

**Competitive map (one line each):** automation agencies push the platform they resell — we're
tool-agnostic. Fractional CTOs are tech-first — we're operations-first. National AI
consultancies won't touch a 12-person design/build firm. DIY/ChatGPT has no method, no safety
rails, and no one accountable in person. Position: **the safe, local, tool-agnostic operator
who is accountable across the table.**

## The portfolio

### WS1 — Legal Shield & Launch Ops — **NOW**
**Objective.** From zero to "can legally, safely take the first dollar": employment-agreement
review, entity + bank, insurance bound, MSA/SOW pack, production site + conversion path live.
**Why now.** Gates everything; ✋ items in `docs/LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` block paid work, and the
conflict question gates the whole company.
**12-month done.** Boring: renewed insurance, clean books, signed MSAs, zero legal surprises.
**Effort.** ~14h month 1, ~2h/mo after. **Depends on.** Attorney availability; lean budget.
**Leading metrics.** Days until first legally-acceptable dollar; checklist §1–4 completion.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Book employment-attorney consult; (2) buy domain + deploy + neutral
mailbox; (3) request 3 tech-E&O/cyber quotes.
**Biggest risk.** The agreement review finds a real prohibition — which is exactly why it goes
first, before money or filings.

### WS2 — Validation GTM: the quiet local engine — **NOW**
**Objective.** Re-plan the 50-touch sprint for a cold start and run it: permit-sourced cold
email at 3× volume, a weekly in-person circuit, a connector top-20 — 10+ fit calls and 3 paid
audits within 60 days of first send.
**Why now.** It *is* the validation stage; nothing else produces evidence or revenue.
**12-month done.** A channel scorecard showing what repeats: ≥3 fit calls/week at known
conversion, founding cohort sold out, standard pricing holding, referral asks firing at every
readout.
**Effort.** ~10h/wk during sprint; ~12h/mo steady. **Depends on.** WS1 (payment/MSA/insurance
before audit #1), WS3 (leave-behinds).
**Leading metrics.** Fit calls booked/week; touches→call rate by channel; audits paid.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Build the 50-prospect list from county permit records + chamber
directory; (2) map the weekly circuit (chamber events, Blue Ridge Home Builders Association,
supplier counters — verify current calendars); (3) draft the connector top-20 with script #4.
**Biggest risk.** Cold conversion below even the re-rated model — the decision tree in the
charter says what changes at 25/50/100 touches.

### WS3 — Firm-Brand Credibility Kit — **NOW**
**Objective.** Public surfaces carry authority with zero founder identifiers; everything a
client touches looks expensive. De-identification copy pass, lean visual-identity pass,
print-grade one-pager + readout/proposal templates, data-posture one-pager, name/trademark
knockout search. Budget ≤$1k.
**Why now.** The site converts cold strangers (no warm network to vouch) and currently exposes
name + named credentials on `/`, `/about`, `/founders-note`, `/apply`, and the printed readout
header — direct conflict with the low-profile constraint.
**12-month done.** Zero name-searchable founder references on public surfaces; a printed kit
that beats every local competitor's paper; the data-posture page cited by prospects.
**Effort.** ~10h month 1, ~2h/mo after. **Depends on.** Identity-policy sign-off (Decision #2).
**Leading metrics.** Grep for identifiers on public routes = 0; kit printed; spend ≤ budget.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Approve the identity policy; (2) execute the copy pass (file list in
the charter — `npm test` must stay green); (3) print the one-pager.
**Biggest risk.** Anonymity read as evasiveness — countered by specific credentials phrased
generically ("founder-led: MBA, ex-global-consultancy AI lead") and heavy in-person presence.

### WS4 — Delivery Engine & Method Hardening — **NEXT** (activates at audit #1)
**Objective.** Run audits 1–3 through the readout/proposal builders, log actual hours per rung,
verify every `needs_review` vendor row a recommendation touches, reprice playbooks from actuals,

**12-month done.** Method priced from data; vendor catalog fully vetted-in-use; Operate cadence

**Effort.** ~4h/mo on top of delivery. **Depends on.** WS2 producing audit #1.
**Leading metrics.** Actual vs. modeled hours; % recommendations on vetted rows.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Add an hours log to `/admin` habit; (2) friction notes after audit #1;
(3) vendor verification for the first recommendation set.
**Biggest risk.** Skipping the logging under time pressure — then pricing stays a guess.

### WS5 — Connector & Partner Channel — **NEXT** (formalizes after 3 audits)
**Objective.** Turn the connector motion (referral script #4) into a named-list program — CPAs,
construction-focused bookkeepers, insurance brokers, bankers, construction attorneys — with a
"safe answer" kit and outcome-reporting loop, so referrals replace cold email as the lead source.
**12-month done.** ≥40% of new fit calls referred; 5+ connectors who have referred twice.
**Effort.** ~6h/mo. **Depends on.** First 3 audits (proof to show), WS3 kit.
**Leading metrics.** Referred fit calls/month; second referrals per connector.
**First 3 actions** (when activated): (1) Promote the top-20 list from WS2 into a tracked
program; (2) build the forwardable kit; (3) institute same-day thank-you + outcome reporting.
**Biggest risk.** Connectors need the firm to feel established — sequencing it after proof
exists is the mitigation.

### WS6 — Earned-Authority Artifacts — **NEXT** (collection now, publishing decision month 6)
**Objective.** Firm-bylined authority that works while the founder stays quiet: the "State of
AI in Owner-Led Businesses — Charlottesville" annual report (data from audits + a 10-minute

associations.
**12-month done.** Report v1 published under the firm byline (if ≥25 survey responses);
2 case studies; workshop delivered ≥2× in private rooms.
**Effort.** ~2h/mo until month 6; ~15h total to publish. **Depends on.** Audits flowing (data),
WS5 (distribution).
**Leading metrics.** Survey responses banked; case-study approvals.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Add 5 benchmark questions to the audit intake habit; (2) draft the
survey; (3) month-6 go/no-go on the report.
**Biggest risk.** Publishing thin data reads as claimed authority — the ≥25-response bar is the
guard; we only do earned.

### WS7 — Economics, Capacity & Pricing Evolution — **NEXT** (lightweight from day 1)
**Objective.** Keep the capacity model honest (hours logged per rung), evolve pricing past the

**12-month done.** Standard pricing validated; a hire/no-hire decision made on data; the

**Effort.** ~2h/mo + gate reviews. **Depends on.** WS4 hour logs.
**Leading metrics.** Effective $/hr per rung; MRR vs. gate targets.
**First 3 actions.** (1) Hours-logging habit (shared with WS4); (2) calendar the month-4 and
month-8 gates now; (3) write the gate criteria into `/admin` pipeline review.
**Biggest risk.** Gates skipped because momentum feels good — calendar them this week.

### WS8 — Custom-Build Practice (Supabase/Next.js margin line) — **LATER**
Doctrine-bound: only after ≥3 clients ask and an audit justifies ROI/risk/scope/budget.
Earliest month 6+. Until then, custom builds are a line in proposals we deliberately decline.

### WS9 — Founder Public Brand & Speaking — **LATER (blocked)**
LinkedIn, podcast, press, public speaking. Unblocks only when the W2 conflict resolves (exit or
explicit clearance). When it unblocks, it inherits a firm with clients, artifacts, and a
referral graph — a better launchpad than going loud now would have been. Revisit at every gate.

### NEVER (doctrine- or evidence-bound)
- Platform/SaaS pivot, white-labeling, implied vendor affiliation, autonomy promises, regulated
  data — doctrine, permanent.
- Vendor-affiliate/referral revenue — it would taint tool-agnosticism, the core positioning.
- Paid ads during validation — lean cash + a trust-sale ICP that doesn't click ads.
- Geographic expansion before Charlottesville is won — criteria to even revisit: ≥8 Operate
  clients and a self-sustaining referral flywheel.
- Productizing anything before ≥3 clients ask — the standing rule.

## Evidence gates & kill criteria

- **Day 30 from first send:** <4 fit calls cumulative → channel problem: shift hours from email
  to the in-person circuit and connectors before touching the offer.
- **Day 60:** <2 paid audits despite ≥10 fit calls → offer/price problem: run the decision tree
  in the GTM charter (objection audit before any price move).
- **Month 4 gate:** 3 paid audits + ≥1 implementation sold → raise cohort 2 to standard pricing.
  Miss → diagnose ICP vs. channel vs. offer with the sprint data; do not silently extend.
- **Month 8 gate:** ≥$4k MRR and audit→implementation ≥40% → W2-exit planning becomes real
  (month 12–18 horizon). Miss → recalibrate to side-income pacing or have the kill conversation.
- **Kill signals (any one):** 100 quality touches + 15 fit calls and <2 audits (offer doesn't
  convert at price); 3 audits delivered and zero implementations (the ladder is broken — audits
  are a product, not a front door); sustained founder hours <8/wk under W2 pressure (capacity
  reality — pause, don't limp).
